( Questions )

The obvious objections.

Why is this paid when everyone else offers a free audit?

Because a free audit is a sales call wearing a costume, and both sides know it. Paying means I work for you rather than for the pitch at the end, and it means I can tell you to do nothing if that's the honest answer. The guarantee puts the risk on me, not you.

I already know what my problems are. What am I paying for?

Most people do know. What they don't have is time to work out which of four hundred tools actually fits a business their size, what it costs, and whether it's worth the setup. That research is the product. Knowing the problem and knowing the fix are different jobs.

Do I need to be technical?

No. If anything it's easier if you're not, because you'll describe the work rather than the software. Nothing in the report assumes you can code, and the four-day plan is written for someone with ten minutes a day.

What if I want you to build the fixes instead of doing it myself?

That's a separate engagement and it starts at $1,500. If you sign one within thirty days, the $299 comes off it. There's no obligation either way, and the report works fine in someone else's hands.

What happens if you don't find five hours?

You get every penny back and you keep the report anyway. I'd rather refund you than argue about whether an hour counts, which is exactly why the three rules are written down.

How soon can we start?

As soon as there's a slot. The call is 45 minutes, the report lands five business days later, and the review is thirty minutes after that. Start to finish it's about a week and a half of calendar time and 75 minutes of yours.
